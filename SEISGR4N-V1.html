SEIS.MONSTER - Sweet Love For Planet Earth sos.allshookup.org

Every M6.0+ earthquake since 2000 is a pin down to its hypocentre: hue = depth, brightness = age. All wave motion stays on or inside the Earth: SE1SFEM, a SPECFEM3D_GLOBE-derived cubed-sphere solver, paints the surface wavefield as vertical velocity (warm up, cool down) and the shakemap as afterglow; the P ray runs through the mantle. Tap one to hear it: the seismogram from the nearest IU station, P−30 s to P+600 s, plays through fourteen instruments while P, S and surface waves cross the Earth in step with the sound.

Each trigger opens with the earthquake itself: the real trace audified (one seismometer sample per audio sample, 20 Hz high-pass), a deep 100× bass strike under a 276× body, P onsets aligned, before SEISREICH and SEISB4RB enter. The gold SEISMONSTER forms on each epicentre and ruptures and disappears on the strike, whose sub-bass is drawn fresh every take: a lower speed-up, a sub tone in A gliding down, never the same twice. Fifteen deep-mantle plumes rise from the core-mantle boundary beneath their hotspots; the nearest plume within 60° of the quake sounds once, a bowed swell tuned to SEISB4RB, when the surface wave reaches it, then falls silent.

SEISGR4N, the starting instrument, is a granular arpeggiator: each quake picks one GRAIN instrument (ROADS, 0PN, S0PHIE, AUT3), which plays for real, and one ARP instrument (B4RB, T3RRY, REICH, KR4FT, APH3X, B0C), which lends its voice. The chord comes from the plate boundary, the register from depth, the speed and range from magnitude, and the chord changes at the S and surface-wave arrivals. Every note is also a grain cloud cut from the real seismogram and pitched to the note. Each earthquake gets a section of about 3 minutes (165–195 s): the seismogram is read once from P wave to coda across it, in four to six movements of uneven length, each a new take that changes on the bar line, and the section builds, peaks and releases before the music moves on to another earthquake, a fresh dataset each time, and no cycle repeats: each take draws new choices and avoids the last pairs and shapes. The SEISGR4N ring around the epicentre is always there: it shows the chord as points on a pitch dial and flashes each note as it plays, and under any other instrument it listens to the music and shows its strongest notes.

SEISSHIFT composes every change instead of cutting it. Each transition draws a few moves, fresh every time: landing on the bar line after a fill bar, keeping the notes two chords share, gliding or pivoting the tempo, handing the high end from the outgoing instrument to the incoming one, dissolving the old music into a grain cloud, previewing the next quake backwards as it swells into its strike, and each instrument's own way in. Distance, magnitude step and the years between two quakes shape how long and how wide the change is, and every instrument is level-matched. Over about ten minutes a long arc thins and darkens the music, then fills and brightens it, so the stream plays as one piece.
